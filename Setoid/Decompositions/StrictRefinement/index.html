    Setoid.Decompositions.StrictRefinement¶

    The strict refinement property and unique factorization¶

    This is the Setoid.Decompositions.StrictRefinement module of the Agda Universal Algebra Library.

    The integers factor uniquely into primes, and so, by the theorem of Wedderburn and Remak, do finite groups into directly indecomposable groups. McKenzie, McNulty, and Taylor, Algebras, Lattices, Varieties I (ALV below), Chapter 5, is the study of when an algebra factors uniquely, in this sense.

    §5.1. We say that 𝑨 has the unique factorization property (UFP), or is uniquely factorable, iff (i) 𝑨 is isomorphic to a product of directly indecomposable algebras, and (ii) this representation is unique; that is, if 𝑨 ≅ ∏_{i∈I} 𝑩ᵢ ≅ ∏_{j∈J} 𝑪ⱼ for some index sets I and J and for directly indecomposable algebras 𝑩ᵢ and 𝑪ⱼ, then there is a bijection φ : I → J such that 𝑩ᵢ ≅ 𝑪_{φ(i)} for all i.

    §5.6. The strict refinement property for 𝑨 asserts that if 0 = ∏_{i∈I} βᵢ = ∏_{j∈J} γⱼ for congruences βᵢ, γⱼ on 𝑨, then there exist congruences δᵢⱼ (i ∈ I, j ∈ J) such that, for all i and j, βᵢ = ∏_{j∈J} δᵢⱼ and γⱼ = ∏_{i∈I} δᵢⱼ.

    Theorem 5.16. If 𝑨 has the strict refinement property, then 𝑨 has the refinement property. If, moreover, 0 = ∏_{i∈I} αᵢ = ∏_{j∈J} βⱼ in Con 𝑨, with each αᵢ and each βⱼ indecomposable, then {αᵢ : i ∈ I} = {βⱼ : j ∈ J}.

    Corollary. If ∏_{i∈I} 𝑩ᵢ has the strict refinement property, all 𝑩ᵢ and all 𝑪ⱼ are directly indecomposable and f : ∏ 𝑩ᵢ → ∏ 𝑪ⱼ is an isomorphism, then there exists a bijection λ : J → I and a family of isomorphisms fⱼ : 𝑩_{λ(j)} → 𝑪ⱼ [such that f acts coordinatewise through them].

    The products here are products of congruences in the sense of ALV §5.2 (Setoid.Decompositions.Products). This module formalizes the following:

    • HasStrictRefinement ι ℓ 𝑨 is the strict refinement property as ALV states it, for index types at a level ι and congruences at level ℓ; HasFiniteStrictRefinement is its restriction to finite index sets, which ALV's Theorem 5.17 (ii) proves equivalent to it, and which follows from it at every level (strict→finite).
    • The second sentence of Theorem 5.16, for finite index sets (srp-unique): two decompositions of 0_A into finitely many indecomposable congruences consist of the same congruences, up to ≑ and a bijection of the indices; so they have the same length (srp-length).
    • Condition (ii) of unique factorization from the strict refinement property, for finite index sets (srp→unique, the Corollary in the form that matters: the factors match up to isomorphism along a bijection).
    • IsUniquelyFactorable, the unique factorization property of §5.1 for finite index sets, and the theorem the section is aiming for: a finite algebra with the strict refinement property is uniquely factorable (finite-srp→uniquelyFactorable, and finitary-srp→uniquelyFactorable over a finite signature), existence coming from Setoid.Decompositions.Finite.

    The index sets are finite (Fin n) because the factors must be found: in an indecomposable congruence αᵢ = ∏ⱼ δᵢⱼ, the one index j with δᵢⱼ ≠ 1 is located by recursion on the list of factors, splitting off one factor at a time by the binary indecomposability of αᵢ. For a finite algebra nothing is lost, since a product of infinitely many nontrivial algebras is infinite; ALV's definition with arbitrary index sets is kept, so that the restriction is visible.

    Two theorems that this chapter of ALV is known for are stated here, with references, but not formalized.

    • Jónsson and Tarski (Direct Decompositions of Finite Algebraic Systems, Notre Dame Mathematical Lectures 5, 1947): every finite algebra with zero is uniquely factorable, where an algebra with zero has a binary operation + and a one-element subuniverse {0} with 0 + x ≈ x + 0 ≈ x (ALV §5.4, the Jónsson–Tarski Unique Factorization Theorem 5.8).
    • Lovász ("Operations with structures", Acta Mathematica Academiae Scientiarum Hungaricae 18, 1967): for finite algebras, 𝑨ᵏ ≅ 𝑩ᵏ with k > 0 implies 𝑨 ≅ 𝑩, and if 𝑪 has a one-element subuniverse then 𝑨 × 𝑪 ≅ 𝑩 × 𝑪 implies 𝑨 ≅ 𝑩 (ALV §5.7, Corollaries 2 and 3 of Theorem 5.23). The cancellation law needs the one-element subuniverse.

    The strict refinement property¶

    HasStrictRefinement ι ℓ 𝑨 is ALV's definition, for families of congruences at level ℓ indexed by types at level ι: any two decompositions 0_A = ∏ β = ∏ γ have a common refinement δ, whose rows decompose the members of β and whose columns decompose the members of γ. These "compatibility conditions" are the point of the definition: each βᵢ is the product of the i-th row, not merely its meet.

    HasFiniteStrictRefinement ℓ 𝑨 asks the same of decompositions indexed by finite sets Fin m and Fin n.

    module _ {𝑆 : Signature 𝓞 𝓥} where

  -- The strict refinement property (ALV §5.6).
  HasStrictRefinement : (ι ℓ : Level) → Algebra {𝑆 = 𝑆} α ρ → Type (α ⊔ ρ ⊔ lsuc ι ⊔ ov {𝑆 = 𝑆} ℓ)
  HasStrictRefinement ι ℓ 𝑨 =
    {I J : Type ι} (β : I → Con 𝑨 ℓ) (γ : J → Con 𝑨 ℓ)
    → IsProduct (Setoid._≈_ 𝔻[ 𝑨 ]) β → IsProduct (Setoid._≈_ 𝔻[ 𝑨 ]) γ
    → Σ[ δ ∈ (I → J → Con 𝑨 ℓ) ]  (∀ i → IsProduct (proj₁ (β i)) (δ i))
                                × (∀ j → IsProduct (proj₁ (γ j)) (λ i → δ i j))

  -- The strict refinement property for finite index sets (ALV Theorem 5.17 (ii)).
  HasFiniteStrictRefinement : (ℓ : Level) → Algebra {𝑆 = 𝑆} α ρ → Type (α ⊔ ρ ⊔ ov {𝑆 = 𝑆} ℓ)
  HasFiniteStrictRefinement ℓ 𝑨 =
    {m n : ℕ} (β : Fin m → Con 𝑨 ℓ) (γ : Fin n → Con 𝑨 ℓ)
    → IsProduct (Setoid._≈_ 𝔻[ 𝑨 ]) β → IsProduct (Setoid._≈_ 𝔻[ 𝑨 ]) γ
    → Σ[ δ ∈ (Fin m → Fin n → Con 𝑨 ℓ) ]  (∀ i → IsProduct (proj₁ (β i)) (δ i))
                                        × (∀ j → IsProduct (proj₁ (γ j)) (λ i → δ i j))

    The property at any index level gives the finite one: a decomposition indexed by Fin m is reindexed by Lift ι (Fin m), refined there, and the refinement is reindexed back (product-reindex, along lift and lower).

      -- The strict refinement property gives its finite form.
  strict→finite : {𝑨 : Algebra {𝑆 = 𝑆} α ρ} → HasStrictRefinement ι ℓ 𝑨 → HasFiniteStrictRefinement ℓ 𝑨
  strict→finite {ι = ι} srp {m} {n} β γ pβ pγ =
    let (δ , pδβ , pδγ) = srp (λ i → β (lower i)) (λ j → γ (lower j))
                              (product-reindex lower lift (λ _ → refl) (λ _ → refl) pβ)
                              (product-reindex lower lift (λ _ → refl) (λ _ → refl) pγ)
    in  (λ i j → δ (lift i) (lift j))
      , (λ i → product-reindex lift lower (λ _ → refl) (λ _ → refl) (pδβ (lift i)))
      , (λ j → product-reindex lift lower (λ _ → refl) (λ _ → refl) (pδγ (lift j)))

    An indecomposable product has one nontrivial factor¶

    The heart of Theorem 5.16 is the following observation. If an indecomposable congruence θ is a product ∏_{j<n} δⱼ, then exactly one factor δⱼ is not total, and θ = δⱼ. Split the product as θ = δ₀ × ⋂_{j>0} δⱼ (product-split); by indecomposability one of the two is total. If the meet of the rest is total, then so is every later factor, and θ = δ₀. If δ₀ is total, then θ is the product of the rest, and we recurse. An empty product would make θ total, contradicting |A/θ| > 1.

    The statement records the index j, that θ ≑ δⱼ, and that every other factor is total.

    module _ {𝑆 : Signature 𝓞 𝓥} {𝑨 : Algebra {𝑆 = 𝑆} α ρ} where

  -- An indecomposable product of a list of congruences has exactly one non-total factor.
  indecomposable-product : {n : ℕ} {θ : Con 𝑨 ℓ} {δ : Fin n → Con 𝑨 ℓ}
    → IsIndecomposable θ → IsProduct (proj₁ θ) δ
    → Σ[ j ∈ Fin n ] (θ ≑ δ j) × (∀ k → k ≢ j → Trivial (𝑨 ╱ δ k))
  indecomposable-product {n = zero} ind p =
    let (a , b , ¬ab) = quotient-nontrivial ind in ⊥-elim (¬ab (product-empty→total p a b))
  indecomposable-product {n = suc n} {θ} {δ} ind p =
    step (product-trivial ind (δ zero) (⋂-tail δ) (product-split p))
    where
    -- An element, for interpolating the shorter product.
    u : 𝕌[ 𝑨 ]
    u = proj₁ (quotient-nontrivial ind)

    -- If δ₀ is total, θ is the product of the remaining factors.
    rest : Trivial (𝑨 ╱ δ zero) → IsProduct (proj₁ θ) (λ k → δ (suc k))
    rest δ₀-total = record
      { to-⋂         = λ r k → to-⋂ p r (suc k)
      ; from-⋂       = λ {x} {y} h → from-⋂ p λ { zero → δ₀-total x y ; (suc k) → h k }
      ; interpolate  = λ a → let (b , ab) = interpolate p λ { zero → u ; (suc k) → a k }
                             in  b , λ k → ab (suc k)
      }

    step : Trivial (𝑨 ╱ δ zero) ⊎ Trivial (𝑨 ╱ ⋂-tail δ)
      → Σ[ j ∈ Fin (suc n) ] (θ ≑ δ j) × (∀ k → k ≢ j → Trivial (𝑨 ╱ δ k))
    step (inj₂ tail-total) =
      zero , ((λ r → to-⋂ p r zero) , λ {x} {y} r → from-⋂ p λ { zero → r
                                                                ; (suc k) → tail-total x y (lift k) })
           , λ { zero 0≢0 → ⊥-elim (0≢0 refl) ; (suc k) _ → λ a b → tail-total a b (lift k) }
    step (inj₁ head-total) =
      let (j , θ≑ , others) = indecomposable-product ind (rest head-total)
      in  suc j , θ≑ , λ { zero _ → head-total
                         ; (suc k) k≢j → others k λ k≡j → k≢j (cong suc k≡j) }

    Theorem 5.16: decompositions into indecomposables coincide¶

    Now let 0_A = ∏_{i<m} αᵢ = ∏_{j<n} βⱼ with every αᵢ and βⱼ indecomposable, and refine the two decompositions by the strict refinement property: αᵢ = ∏ⱼ δᵢⱼ and βⱼ = ∏ᵢ δᵢⱼ. By the lemma, each row i has exactly one non-total entry, in column σ i, with αᵢ ≑ δ_{i,σ i}; and each column j has exactly one non-total entry, in row τ j, with βⱼ ≑ δ_{τ j,j}. The entry δ_{i,σ i} is not total, because αᵢ is not; so it is the one non-total entry of column σ i, and τ (σ i) = i. Symmetrically σ (τ j) = j. So σ is a bijection, and αᵢ ≑ δ_{i,σ i} ≑ β_{σ i}: the two decompositions consist of the same congruences, which is the second sentence of Theorem 5.16 (ALV writes it as the equation of sets {αᵢ} = {βⱼ}).

      -- ALV Theorem 5.16, second sentence (finite index sets): two decompositions of the
  -- diagonal into indecomposable congruences consist of the same congruences.
  srp-unique : HasFiniteStrictRefinement ℓ 𝑨
    → {m n : ℕ} (αs : Fin m → Con 𝑨 ℓ) (βs : Fin n → Con 𝑨 ℓ)
    → IsProduct (Setoid._≈_ 𝔻[ 𝑨 ]) αs → IsProduct (Setoid._≈_ 𝔻[ 𝑨 ]) βs
    → (∀ i → IsIndecomposable (αs i)) → (∀ j → IsIndecomposable (βs j))
    → Σ[ σ ∈ Permutation m n ] (∀ i → αs i ≑ βs (σ ⟨$⟩ʳ i))
  srp-unique srp {m} {n} αs βs pα pβ indα indβ = permutation σ τ στ τσ , matched
    where
    refinement = srp αs βs pα pβ
    δ = proj₁ refinement

    row : ∀ i → Σ[ j ∈ _ ] (αs i ≑ δ i j) × (∀ k → k ≢ j → Trivial (𝑨 ╱ δ i k))
    row i = indecomposable-product (indα i) (proj₁ (proj₂ refinement) i)

    column : ∀ j → Σ[ i ∈ _ ] (βs j ≑ δ i j) × (∀ k → k ≢ i → Trivial (𝑨 ╱ δ k j))
    column j = indecomposable-product (indβ j) (proj₂ (proj₂ refinement) j)

    σ : Fin m → Fin n
    σ i = proj₁ (row i)

    τ : Fin n → Fin m
    τ j = proj₁ (column j)

    -- The entry at row i, column σ i, is not total, since αᵢ is not.
    row-entry-nontrivial : ∀ i → Trivial (𝑨 ╱ δ i (σ i)) → Trivial (𝑨 ╱ αs i)
    row-entry-nontrivial i t a b = proj₂ (proj₁ (proj₂ (row i))) (t a b)

    column-entry-nontrivial : ∀ j → Trivial (𝑨 ╱ δ (τ j) j) → Trivial (𝑨 ╱ βs j)
    column-entry-nontrivial j t a b = proj₂ (proj₁ (proj₂ (column j))) (t a b)

    -- So it is the one non-total entry of its column: τ (σ i) = i.
    τσ : ∀ i → τ (σ i) ≡ i
    τσ i with τ (σ i) ≟ i
    ... | yes eq = eq
    ... | no ne  =
      let (a , b , ¬ab) = quotient-nontrivial (indα i)
      in  ⊥-elim (¬ab (row-entry-nontrivial i (proj₂ (proj₂ (column (σ i))) i (λ e → ne (sym e))) a b))

    -- Symmetrically, σ (τ j) = j.
    στ : ∀ j → σ (τ j) ≡ j
    στ j with σ (τ j) ≟ j
    ... | yes eq = eq
    ... | no ne  =
      let (a , b , ¬ab) = quotient-nontrivial (indβ j)
      in  ⊥-elim (¬ab (column-entry-nontrivial j (proj₂ (proj₂ (row (τ j))) j (λ e → ne (sym e))) a b))

    -- αᵢ ≑ δ_{i,σ i} ≑ β_{σ i}.
    matched : ∀ i → αs i ≑ βs (σ i)
    matched i = (λ r → proj₂ β≑δ (proj₁ α≑δ r)) , λ r → proj₂ α≑δ (proj₁ β≑δ r)
      where
      α≑δ : αs i ≑ δ i (σ i)
      α≑δ = proj₁ (proj₂ (row i))
      β≑δ : βs (σ i) ≑ δ i (σ i)
      β≑δ = subst (λ k → βs (σ i) ≑ δ k (σ i)) (τσ i) (proj₁ (proj₂ (column (σ i))))

  -- In particular the two decompositions have the same length.
  srp-length : HasFiniteStrictRefinement ℓ 𝑨
    → {m n : ℕ} (αs : Fin m → Con 𝑨 ℓ) (βs : Fin n → Con 𝑨 ℓ)
    → IsProduct (Setoid._≈_ 𝔻[ 𝑨 ]) αs → IsProduct (Setoid._≈_ 𝔻[ 𝑨 ]) βs
    → (∀ i → IsIndecomposable (αs i)) → (∀ j → IsIndecomposable (βs j))
    → m ≡ n
  srp-length srp αs βs pα pβ indα indβ = ↔⇒≡ (proj₁ (srp-unique srp αs βs pα pβ indα indβ))

    Unique factorization¶

    The Corollary of Theorem 5.16 transfers the theorem from congruences to algebras. Given 𝑨 ≅ ⨅ ℬ ≅ ⨅ 𝒞 with directly indecomposable factors, the kernels of the coordinate maps decompose the diagonal (≅⨅→product), each factor is the quotient by its kernel (≅⨅-factor≅), so each kernel is indecomposable (invariance under isomorphism and Lemma 2 of §5.2); Theorem 5.16 matches the kernels along a bijection σ, and matched kernels have isomorphic quotients (≑→╱≅). So ℬ i ≅ 𝑨/κᵢ ≅ 𝑨/λ_{σ i} ≅ 𝒞 (σ i), which is condition (ii) of the unique factorization property. (Recovering the factors as quotients takes an element of each factor, which nontriviality supplies.)

    module _ {𝑆 : Signature 𝓞 𝓥} {𝑨 : Algebra {𝑆 = 𝑆} α ρ} where

  -- (ALV §5.1 (ii), Theorem 5.16 and its Corollary, finite index sets.)  If 𝑨 has the
  -- strict refinement property, two factorizations of 𝑨 into directly indecomposable
  -- algebras match up to isomorphism along a bijection of their indices.
  srp→unique : HasFiniteStrictRefinement ρᵇ 𝑨
    → {m n : ℕ} (ℬ : Fin m → Algebra {𝑆 = 𝑆} β ρᵇ) (𝒞 : Fin n → Algebra {𝑆 = 𝑆} β ρᵇ)
    → 𝑨 ≅ ⨅ ℬ → 𝑨 ≅ ⨅ 𝒞
    → (∀ i → IsDirectlyIndecomposable ρᵇ (ℬ i)) → (∀ j → IsDirectlyIndecomposable ρᵇ (𝒞 j))
    → Σ[ σ ∈ Permutation m n ] (∀ i → ℬ i ≅ 𝒞 (σ ⟨$⟩ʳ i))
  srp→unique srp ℬ 𝒞 f g indℬ ind𝒞 =
    let (σ , matched) = srp-unique srp (≅⨅-ker f) (≅⨅-ker g) (≅⨅→product f) (≅⨅→product g)
                                   indκ indλ
    in  σ , λ i → ≅-trans (≅-trans (ℬ≅ i) (≑→╱≅ (matched i))) (𝒞≅ (σ ⟨$⟩ʳ i))
    where
    -- Each factor is the quotient by the kernel of its coordinate map.
    ℬ≅ : ∀ i → ℬ i ≅ (𝑨 ╱ ≅⨅-ker f i)
    ℬ≅ i = ≅-sym (≅⨅-factor≅ f _≟_ (λ k → proj₁ (nontrivial (indℬ k))) i)

    𝒞≅ : ∀ j → (𝑨 ╱ ≅⨅-ker g j) ≅ 𝒞 j
    𝒞≅ j = ≅⨅-factor≅ g _≟_ (λ k → proj₁ (nontrivial (ind𝒞 k))) j

    -- So the kernels are indecomposable congruences.
    indκ : ∀ i → IsIndecomposable (≅⨅-ker f i)
    indκ i = directly→indecomposable (≅⨅-ker f i) (≅-indecomposable (ℬ≅ i) (indℬ i))

    indλ : ∀ j → IsIndecomposable (≅⨅-ker g j)
    indλ j = directly→indecomposable (≅⨅-ker g j) (≅-indecomposable (≅-sym (𝒞≅ j)) (ind𝒞 j))

    IsUniquelyFactorable β ρᵇ 𝑨 is the unique factorization property of ALV §5.1, for finite index sets and for factors at the levels (β , ρᵇ): a factorization into directly indecomposable algebras exists (factorization), and any two such factorizations match up to isomorphism along a bijection of their indices (uniqueness).

    A finite algebra has a factorization, by Setoid.Decompositions.Finite, into quotients of itself, which live at the levels (α , W) with W = 𝓞 ⊔ 𝓥 ⊔ α ⊔ ρ the working level of the congruence list; with the strict refinement property at that level it is uniquely factorable. This is the theorem of the section: a finite algebra with the strict refinement property is uniquely factorable.

    module _ {𝑆 : Signature 𝓞 𝓥} where
  -- The unique factorization property (ALV §5.1), for finite index sets.
  record IsUniquelyFactorable (β ρᵇ : Level) (𝑨 : Algebra {𝑆 = 𝑆} α ρ)
    : Type (α ⊔ ρ ⊔ ov {𝑆 = 𝑆} (β ⊔ ρᵇ)) where
    field
      factorization  : Σ[ n ∈ ℕ ] Σ[ ℬ ∈ (Fin n → Algebra {𝑆 = 𝑆} β ρᵇ) ]
                         (𝑨 ≅ ⨅ ℬ) × (∀ i → IsDirectlyIndecomposable ρᵇ (ℬ i))
      uniqueness     : {m n : ℕ} (ℬ : Fin m → Algebra {𝑆 = 𝑆} β ρᵇ) (𝒞 : Fin n → Algebra {𝑆 = 𝑆} β ρᵇ)
                       → 𝑨 ≅ ⨅ ℬ → 𝑨 ≅ ⨅ 𝒞
                       → (∀ i → IsDirectlyIndecomposable ρᵇ (ℬ i))
                       → (∀ j → IsDirectlyIndecomposable ρᵇ (𝒞 j))
                       → Σ[ σ ∈ Permutation m n ] (∀ i → ℬ i ≅ 𝒞 (σ ⟨$⟩ʳ i))

  open IsUniquelyFactorable public

module _ {𝑆 : Signature 𝓞 𝓥} {𝑨 : Algebra {𝑆 = 𝑆} α ρ} where
  -- A finite algebra with the strict refinement property is uniquely factorable.
  finite-srp→uniquelyFactorable : FiniteAlgebra 𝑨 → FiniteCongruencesᵈ 𝑨 → 𝕌[ 𝑨 ]
    → HasFiniteStrictRefinement (𝓞 ⊔ 𝓥 ⊔ α ⊔ ρ) 𝑨 → IsUniquelyFactorable α (𝓞 ⊔ 𝓥 ⊔ α ⊔ ρ) 𝑨
  finite-srp→uniquelyFactorable 𝑭 𝑪 u srp = record
    { factorization  = let (n , θ , iso , ind) = finite-decomposition≅ 𝑭 𝑪 u in n , 𝑨╱ θ , iso , ind
    ; uniqueness     = srp→unique srp
    }

    Over a finite signature the complete list always exists (Setoid.Decompositions.Finite), so the theorem needs only the finiteness of the algebra and of its signature.

      -- A nonempty finite finitary algebra with the strict refinement property is
  -- uniquely factorable.
  finitary-srp→uniquelyFactorable : FiniteAlgebra 𝑨 → FiniteSignature 𝑆 → 𝕌[ 𝑨 ]
    → HasFiniteStrictRefinement (𝓞 ⊔ 𝓥 ⊔ α ⊔ ρ) 𝑨 → IsUniquelyFactorable α (𝓞 ⊔ 𝓥 ⊔ α ⊔ ρ) 𝑨
  finitary-srp→uniquelyFactorable 𝑭 𝑺 =
    finite-srp→uniquelyFactorable 𝑭 (FiniteAlgebra→FiniteCongruencesᵈ 𝑭 𝑺)
