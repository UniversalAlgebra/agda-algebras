    Setoid.Decompositions.FactorPairs¶

    Factor pairs of congruences¶

    This is the Setoid.Decompositions.FactorPairs module of the Agda Universal Algebra Library.

    An algebra 𝑨 is a direct product 𝑩 × 𝑪 exactly when two of its congruences split it into two independent coordinates. McKenzie, McNulty, and Taylor make this precise in Algebras, Lattices, Varieties I (ALV below), §4.4.

    Definition 4.28 (2, 3). Let α and β be equivalence relations on a set A. We write 0_A = α × β and call ⟨α, β⟩ a pair of complementary factor relations iff α ∧ β = 0_A and α ∨ β = 1_A and moreover α and β permute. Let α and β be congruences of an algebra 𝑨. Then ⟨α, β⟩ is a pair of complementary factor congruences of 𝑨 iff 0_A = α × β.

    Theorem 4.29. A pair ⟨φ₀, φ₁⟩ of congruences of an algebra gives a direct representation iff 0_A = φ₀ × φ₁.

    Here "gives a direct representation" means that the natural map x ↦ (x/φ₀ , x/φ₁) is an isomorphism 𝑨 ≅ 𝑨/φ₀ × 𝑨/φ₁, and ALV's proof is the observation that the map is injective iff φ₀ ∧ φ₁ = 0_A and onto iff φ₀ ∘ φ₁ = 1_A. This module formalizes the definition, the theorem, and its converse.

    • IsFactorPair is Definition 4.28, with its three conditions: the meet is the diagonal, the join is the total relation, and the two congruences permute (Permutes of Setoid.Congruences.Permutability).
    • IsProduct₂ R θ φ is the binary case of the product of congruences of ALV §5.2: ψ = θ × φ means that ψ = θ ∧ φ and θ ∘ φ = 1_A. For ψ = 0_A it is the composite form of a factor pair, in which the join, a generated congruence, is replaced by the composite, a relation one can compute with; factorPair→product₂ and product₂→factorPair prove the two forms equivalent.
    • factorPair→≅ is Theorem 4.29 from right to left, 𝑨 ≅ (𝑨 ╱ θ) ×ᴬ (𝑨 ╱ φ), and natmap₂-bijective→factorPair is its converse.
    • ≅×→factorPair goes the other way round: for any isomorphism 𝑨 ≅ 𝑩 ×ᴬ 𝑪, the kernels of the two coordinate maps form a factor pair, and 𝑩 and 𝑪 are the quotients by them (≅×-factor≅ˡ, ≅×-factor≅ʳ).
    • product₂-decˡ records a constructive fact that the finite theory of Setoid.Decompositions.Finite rests on: if ψ = θ × φ and ψ is decidable, then so is θ, given one element of the carrier. In particular the factor congruences of an algebra with decidable equality are decidable, with no finiteness or classical assumption.

    The products of arbitrary families of congruences are in Setoid.Decompositions.Products, and indecomposability in Setoid.Decompositions.Indecomposable.

    The product of two congruences¶

    Fix an algebra 𝑨 and congruences θ and φ. ALV §5.2 calls a congruence ψ the product of θ and φ, written ψ = θ × φ, when the natural map 𝑨/ψ → 𝑨/θ × 𝑨/φ is a bijection; Lemma 1 there shows that this happens exactly when ψ = θ ∧ φ and θ ∘ φ = 1_A ("that θ₀ and θ₁ permute, and that their join is 1"). We take that characterization as the definition, stated for the relation R of ψ so that ψ may be the diagonal 0_A, which is the setoid equality _≈_ itself.

    The record IsProduct₂ R θ φ therefore has three fields: R is contained in the meet (to-meet), the meet is contained in R (from-meet), and every pair is related by the composite (composite), whose witness z with x θ z φ y is the element "with the first coordinate of x and the second coordinate of y".

    module _ {𝑆 : Signature 𝓞 𝓥} {𝑨 : Algebra {𝑆 = 𝑆} α ρ} where

  -- ψ = θ × φ (ALV §5.2), for the relation R of ψ: R = θ ∧ φ and θ ∘ φ = 1_A.
  record IsProduct₂ (R : BinaryRel 𝕌[ 𝑨 ] ℓ′) (θ φ : Con 𝑨 ℓ) : Type (α ⊔ ℓ ⊔ ℓ′) where
    field
      to-meet    : ∀ {x y} → R x y → proj₁ (θ ∧ φ) x y
      from-meet  : ∀ {x y} → proj₁ (θ ∧ φ) x y → R x y
      composite  : ∀ x y → (θ ∘ φ) x y

  open IsProduct₂ public

    A factor pair in the sense of Definition 4.28 is a pair of congruences whose meet is the diagonal, whose join is the total relation, and which permute. The join _∨_ is the generated congruence of Setoid.Congruences.Generation and permutation is the single inclusion Permutes, which implies the textbook equation by Permutes-sym.

      -- ⟨θ , φ⟩ is a pair of complementary factor congruences (ALV Definition 4.28).
  record IsFactorPair (θ φ : Con 𝑨 ℓ) : Type (𝓞 ⊔ 𝓥 ⊔ α ⊔ ρ ⊔ ℓ) where
    field
      meet-zero  : BelowDiagonal 𝑨 (θ ∧ φ)               -- θ ∧ φ = 0_A
      join-one   : ∀ x y → proj₁ (θ ∨ φ) x y             -- θ ∨ φ = 1_A
      permute    : Permutes θ φ                          -- θ ∘ φ = φ ∘ θ

  open IsFactorPair public

    The two forms of a factor pair¶

    The composite of a factor pair is the total relation, because the two congruences permute and so their join is their composite (permutes→∨⊆∘ of Setoid.Congruences.Permutability). Conversely a total composite gives the total join, which contains it (∘⊆∨), and permutation, since then both composites are total. So a factor pair is the same thing as a product decomposition 0_A = θ × φ of the diagonal; this is ALV's remark after Definition 4.28 and the content of its proof of Theorem 4.29.

    The swapped composite φ ∘ θ of a product is total along with θ ∘ φ, since the converse of a composite of congruences is the composite in the other order (composite-swap).

    module _ {𝑆 : Signature 𝓞 𝓥} {𝑨 : Algebra {𝑆 = 𝑆} α ρ} where
  open Setoid 𝔻[ 𝑨 ] using ( _≈_ )
  private variable θ θ′ φ φ′ : Con 𝑨 ℓ

  -- In ψ = θ × φ, the composite in the other order is total too.
  composite-swap : {R : BinaryRel 𝕌[ 𝑨 ] ℓ′} → IsProduct₂ R θ φ → ∀ x y → (φ ∘ θ) x y
  composite-swap {θ = θ} {φ = φ} p x y =
    let (z , yθz , zφx) = composite p y x
    in  z , IsEquivalence.sym (is-equivalence (proj₂ φ)) zφx
          , IsEquivalence.sym (is-equivalence (proj₂ θ)) yθz

  -- The diagonal is below every meet of congruences.
  ≈→meet : ∀ {x y} → x ≈ y → proj₁ (θ ∧ φ) x y
  ≈→meet {θ = θ} {φ = φ} x≈y = reflexive (proj₂ θ) x≈y , reflexive (proj₂ φ) x≈y

  -- A factor pair (Definition 4.28) is a product decomposition 0_A = θ × φ.
  factorPair→product₂ : IsFactorPair θ φ → IsProduct₂ _≈_ θ φ
  factorPair→product₂ {θ = θ} {φ = φ} fp = record
    { to-meet    = ≈→meet {θ = θ} {φ = φ}
    ; from-meet  = meet-zero fp
    ; composite  = λ x y → permutes→∨⊆∘ θ φ (permute fp) (join-one fp x y)
    }

  -- A product decomposition 0_A = θ × φ is a factor pair.
  product₂→factorPair : IsProduct₂ _≈_ θ φ → IsFactorPair θ φ
  product₂→factorPair {θ = θ} {φ = φ} p = record
    { meet-zero  = from-meet p
    ; join-one   = λ x y → ∘⊆∨ θ φ (composite p x y)
    ; permute    = λ {x} {y} _ → composite-swap p x y
    }

    Both forms are symmetric in the two factors, and both respect the equality _≑_ of congruences (mutual containment); the product form also respects replacing R by an equivalent relation. product₂-sym, factorPair-sym, product₂-≑, and product₂-resp record these facts.

      -- ψ = θ × φ implies ψ = φ × θ.
  product₂-sym : {R : BinaryRel 𝕌[ 𝑨 ] ℓ′} → IsProduct₂ R θ φ → IsProduct₂ R φ θ
  product₂-sym p = record
    { to-meet    = λ r → swap (to-meet p r)
    ; from-meet  = λ m → from-meet p (swap m)
    ; composite  = composite-swap p
    }

  -- ⟨θ , φ⟩ is a factor pair iff ⟨φ , θ⟩ is.
  factorPair-sym : IsFactorPair θ φ → IsFactorPair φ θ
  factorPair-sym fp = product₂→factorPair (product₂-sym (factorPair→product₂ fp))

  -- The product of two congruences respects ≑ in each factor.
  product₂-≑ : {R : BinaryRel 𝕌[ 𝑨 ] ℓ′}
    → θ ≑ θ′ → φ ≑ φ′ → IsProduct₂ R θ φ → IsProduct₂ R θ′ φ′
  product₂-≑ (θ⊆θ′ , θ′⊆θ) (φ⊆φ′ , φ′⊆φ) p = record
    { to-meet    = λ r → let (a , b) = to-meet p r in θ⊆θ′ a , φ⊆φ′ b
    ; from-meet  = λ (a , b) → from-meet p (θ′⊆θ a , φ′⊆φ b)
    ; composite  = λ x y → let (z , a , b) = composite p x y in z , θ⊆θ′ a , φ⊆φ′ b
    }

  -- The product of two congruences respects equivalence of the relation R.
  product₂-resp : {R : BinaryRel 𝕌[ 𝑨 ] ℓ′}{R′ : BinaryRel 𝕌[ 𝑨 ] ℓ″}
    → R ⇒ R′ → R′ ⇒ R → IsProduct₂ R θ φ → IsProduct₂ R′ θ φ
  product₂-resp R⇒R′ R′⇒R p = record
    { to-meet    = λ r → to-meet p (R′⇒R r)
    ; from-meet  = λ m → R⇒R′ (from-meet p m)
    ; composite  = composite p
    }

    In a product ψ = θ × φ one factor is trivial exactly when the other is already below ψ: if 𝑨 ╱ θ is trivial then φ = θ ∧ φ ⊆ ψ, and if φ ⊆ ψ then every x θ z φ y gives z ψ y, hence z θ y and x θ y. For a factor pair (ψ = 0_A) this says that θ = 1_A iff φ = 0_A: the trivial factor of a direct decomposition is the one whose complement is the diagonal.

      -- If 𝑨 ╱ θ is trivial, then φ is below ψ.
  product₂-trivialˡ→ : {R : BinaryRel 𝕌[ 𝑨 ] ℓ′}
    → IsProduct₂ R θ φ → Trivial (𝑨 ╱ θ) → proj₁ φ ⇒ R
  product₂-trivialˡ→ p θ1 {x} {y} xφy = from-meet p (θ1 x y , xφy)

  -- If φ is below ψ, then 𝑨 ╱ θ is trivial.
  product₂-→trivialˡ : {R : BinaryRel 𝕌[ 𝑨 ] ℓ′}
    → IsProduct₂ R θ φ → proj₁ φ ⇒ R → Trivial (𝑨 ╱ θ)
  product₂-→trivialˡ {θ = θ} p φ⇒R x y =
    let (z , xθz , zφy) = composite p x y
    in  IsEquivalence.trans (is-equivalence (proj₂ θ)) xθz (proj₁ (to-meet p (φ⇒R zφy)))

    Theorem 4.29: the decomposition isomorphism¶

    The natural map natmap₂ θ φ sends x to the pair of its classes, (x/θ , x/φ); it is the pairing (×-hom-co) of the two quotient maps. Two elements have the same image exactly when they are related by both θ and φ, so the map is injective iff θ ∧ φ = 0_A; this holds definitionally, as in the subdirect case of Setoid.Subalgebras.Subdirect.Basic. A pair of classes (a/θ , b/φ) has a preimage z exactly when a θ z φ b, so the map is onto iff θ ∘ φ = 1_A.

    module _ {𝑆 : Signature 𝓞 𝓥} {𝑨 : Algebra {𝑆 = 𝑆} α ρ} (θ φ : Con 𝑨 ℓ) where
  open Setoid 𝔻[ 𝑨 ] using ( _≈_ )
  private
    φsym : ∀ {x y} → proj₁ φ x y → proj₁ φ y x
    φsym = IsEquivalence.sym (is-equivalence (proj₂ φ))

  -- The natural map x ↦ (x/θ , x/φ).
  natmap₂ : hom 𝑨 ((𝑨 ╱ θ) ×ᴬ (𝑨 ╱ φ))
  natmap₂ = ×-hom-co (πhom 𝒾𝒹 θ) (πhom 𝒾𝒹 φ)

  -- Injectivity of the natural map is the condition θ ∧ φ = 0_A, definitionally.
  natmap₂-injective : BelowDiagonal 𝑨 (θ ∧ φ) → IsInjective (proj₁ natmap₂)
  natmap₂-injective = id

  natmap₂-injective⁻ : IsInjective (proj₁ natmap₂) → BelowDiagonal 𝑨 (θ ∧ φ)
  natmap₂-injective⁻ = id

  -- The natural map is onto when θ ∘ φ = 1_A ...
  natmap₂-surjective : (∀ x y → (θ ∘ φ) x y) → IsSurjective (proj₁ natmap₂)
  natmap₂-surjective c {a , b} = let (z , aθz , zφb) = c a b in Image_∋_.eq z (aθz , φsym zφb)

  -- ... and only then.
  natmap₂-surjective⁻ : IsSurjective (proj₁ natmap₂) → ∀ x y → (θ ∘ φ) x y
  natmap₂-surjective⁻ s x y = preimage (s {x , y})
    where
    preimage : Image (proj₁ natmap₂) ∋ (x , y) → (θ ∘ φ) x y
    preimage (Image_∋_.eq z (xθz , yφz)) = z , xθz , φsym yφz

    Theorem 4.29 follows: a factor pair makes the natural map a bijective homomorphism, hence an isomorphism 𝑨 ≅ (𝑨 ╱ θ) ×ᴬ (𝑨 ╱ φ) (factorPair→≅), and conversely a bijective natural map makes the pair a factor pair (natmap₂-bijective→factorPair).

      -- ALV Theorem 4.29, (⇐): a factor pair gives the direct representation 𝑨 ≅ 𝑨/θ × 𝑨/φ.
  factorPair→≅ : IsFactorPair θ φ → 𝑨 ≅ ((𝑨 ╱ θ) ×ᴬ (𝑨 ╱ φ))
  factorPair→≅ fp = Bijective→≅ natmap₂  (natmap₂-injective (meet-zero fp))
                                         (natmap₂-surjective (composite (factorPair→product₂ fp)))

  -- ALV Theorem 4.29, (⇒): if the natural map is bijective, ⟨θ , φ⟩ is a factor pair.
  natmap₂-bijective→factorPair : IsInjective (proj₁ natmap₂) → IsSurjective (proj₁ natmap₂)
    → IsFactorPair θ φ
  natmap₂-bijective→factorPair inj surj = product₂→factorPair record
    { to-meet    = ≈→meet {θ = θ} {φ = φ}
    ; from-meet  = natmap₂-injective⁻ inj
    ; composite  = natmap₂-surjective⁻ surj
    }

    Every direct decomposition comes from a factor pair¶

    Conversely, let f : 𝑨 ≅ 𝑩 ×ᴬ 𝑪 be any isomorphism onto a binary product. The kernels ≅×-kerˡ f and ≅×-kerʳ f of its two coordinate maps form a factor pair (ALV §5.2: "an isomorphism f : 𝑨 → ∏ 𝑩ᵢ is determined within isomorphism by certain congruences αᵢ on 𝑨, namely αᵢ = ker pᵢ ∘ f"). Their meet is the kernel of f itself, the diagonal; and any two elements x and y are linked by the element z whose image has the first coordinate of f x and the second coordinate of f y. The two factors are taken at the same levels, so that the two kernels are congruences at one level.

    module _ {𝑆 : Signature 𝓞 𝓥} {𝑨 : Algebra {𝑆 = 𝑆} α ρ} {𝑩 𝑪 : Algebra {𝑆 = 𝑆} β ρᵇ}
         (f : 𝑨 ≅ (𝑩 ×ᴬ 𝑪)) where
  open Setoid 𝔻[ 𝑩 ] using () renaming ( sym to symᵇ )

  -- The coordinate maps of f.
  ≅×-coordˡ : hom 𝑨 𝑩
  ≅×-coordˡ = ⊙-hom (to f) ×-proj₁

  ≅×-coordʳ : hom 𝑨 𝑪
  ≅×-coordʳ = ⊙-hom (to f) ×-proj₂

  -- Their kernels, ker (p₁ ∘ f) and ker (p₂ ∘ f).
  ≅×-kerˡ : Con 𝑨 ρᵇ
  ≅×-kerˡ = kercon ≅×-coordˡ

  ≅×-kerʳ : Con 𝑨 ρᵇ
  ≅×-kerʳ = kercon ≅×-coordʳ

  -- The kernels of the coordinate maps of 𝑨 ≅ 𝑩 × 𝑪 decompose the diagonal.
  ≅×→product₂ : IsProduct₂ (Setoid._≈_ 𝔻[ 𝑨 ]) ≅×-kerˡ ≅×-kerʳ
  ≅×→product₂ = record
    { to-meet    = λ x≈y → cong (proj₁ ≅×-coordˡ) x≈y , cong (proj₁ ≅×-coordʳ) x≈y
    ; from-meet  = ≅toInjective f
    ; composite  = linked
    }
    where
    linked : ∀ x y → (≅×-kerˡ ∘ ≅×-kerʳ) x y
    linked x y = z , Setoid.sym 𝔻[ 𝑩 ] (proj₁ fz≈) , proj₂ fz≈
      where
      z = from f .proj₁ ⟨$⟩ (proj₁ (to f .proj₁ ⟨$⟩ x) , proj₂ (to f .proj₁ ⟨$⟩ y))
      fz≈ = to∼from f (proj₁ (to f .proj₁ ⟨$⟩ x) , proj₂ (to f .proj₁ ⟨$⟩ y))

  -- ... so they form a factor pair.
  ≅×→factorPair : IsFactorPair ≅×-kerˡ ≅×-kerʳ
  ≅×→factorPair = product₂→factorPair ≅×→product₂

    Each factor is recovered as the quotient by the kernel of its coordinate map, by the first isomorphism theorem (FirstIsoTheorem of Setoid.Homomorphisms.Noether), once the coordinate map is known to be onto. The first coordinate map hits b at the preimage of (b , c), for any element c of the other factor, so each statement takes an element of the other factor. (Some element is needed: if 𝑪 is empty then so is 𝑨, while 𝑩 need not be.)

      -- 𝑩 ≅ 𝑨 ╱ ker (p₁ ∘ f), given an element of 𝑪.
  ≅×-factor≅ˡ : 𝕌[ 𝑪 ] → (𝑨 ╱ ≅×-kerˡ) ≅ 𝑩
  ≅×-factor≅ˡ c = FirstIsoTheorem ≅×-coordˡ onto
    where
    onto : IsSurjective (proj₁ ≅×-coordˡ)
    onto {b} = Image_∋_.eq (from f .proj₁ ⟨$⟩ (b , c)) (symᵇ (proj₁ (to∼from f (b , c))))

  -- 𝑪 ≅ 𝑨 ╱ ker (p₂ ∘ f), given an element of 𝑩.
  ≅×-factor≅ʳ : 𝕌[ 𝑩 ] → (𝑨 ╱ ≅×-kerʳ) ≅ 𝑪
  ≅×-factor≅ʳ b = FirstIsoTheorem ≅×-coordʳ onto
    where
    onto : IsSurjective (proj₁ ≅×-coordʳ)
    onto {c} = Image_∋_.eq (from f .proj₁ ⟨$⟩ (b , c))
                           (Setoid.sym 𝔻[ 𝑪 ] (proj₂ (to∼from f (b , c))))

    Factor congruences are decidable¶

    Fix a product ψ = θ × φ and an element u. Every x determines the element coordˡx with x θ coordˡ x φ u; think of it as the element with the θ-coordinate of x and the φ-coordinate of u. Then x θ y holds exactly when coordˡ x ψ coordˡ y: the two are θ-related through x and y, and φ-related through u, so related by θ ∧ φ = ψ; and conversely ψ ⊆ θ. So θ is decidable as soon as ψ is, and the decision costs one evaluation of ψ. With ψ = 0_A this says that the factor congruences of an algebra with decidable equality are decidable.

    The point matters for finite algebras. Their semantic congruences need not be decidable (see ADR-008 and Setoid.Congruences.Finite.Basic), so a complete list of decidable congruences says nothing, in general, about the semantic ones. But it does say everything about the factor pairs, which is what Setoid.Decompositions.Finite uses to decide direct indecomposability with no classical assumption.

    module _ {𝑆 : Signature 𝓞 𝓥} {𝑨 : Algebra {𝑆 = 𝑆} α ρ} {R : BinaryRel 𝕌[ 𝑨 ] ℓ′}
         {θ φ : Con 𝑨 ℓ} (p : IsProduct₂ R θ φ) (u : 𝕌[ 𝑨 ]) where
  private
    θsym = IsEquivalence.sym (is-equivalence (proj₂ θ))
    θtrans = IsEquivalence.trans (is-equivalence (proj₂ θ))
    φsym = IsEquivalence.sym (is-equivalence (proj₂ φ))
    φtrans = IsEquivalence.trans (is-equivalence (proj₂ φ))

  -- The element with the θ-coordinate of x and the φ-coordinate of u.
  coordˡ : 𝕌[ 𝑨 ] → 𝕌[ 𝑨 ]
  coordˡ x = proj₁ (composite p x u)

  -- θ-related elements have ψ-related coordinates ...
  θ→coordˡ : ∀ {x y} → proj₁ θ x y → R (coordˡ x) (coordˡ y)
  θ→coordˡ {x} {y} xθy =
    let (_ , xθx′ , x′φu) = composite p x u
        (_ , yθy′ , y′φu) = composite p y u
    in  from-meet p ( θtrans (θsym xθx′) (θtrans xθy yθy′)
                    , φtrans x′φu (φsym y′φu) )

  -- ... and conversely.
  coordˡ→θ : ∀ {x y} → R (coordˡ x) (coordˡ y) → proj₁ θ x y
  coordˡ→θ {x} {y} r =
    let (_ , xθx′ , _) = composite p x u
        (_ , yθy′ , _) = composite p y u
    in  θtrans xθx′ (θtrans (proj₁ (to-meet p r)) (θsym yθy′))

  -- If ψ is decidable, then so is its factor θ.
  product₂-decˡ : (∀ x y → Dec (R x y)) → ∀ x y → Dec (proj₁ θ x y)
  product₂-decˡ R? x y = map′ coordˡ→θ θ→coordˡ (R? (coordˡ x) (coordˡ y))

    By symmetry the same holds for the other factor.

    module _ {𝑆 : Signature 𝓞 𝓥} {𝑨 : Algebra {𝑆 = 𝑆} α ρ} {R : BinaryRel 𝕌[ 𝑨 ] ℓ′}
         {θ φ : Con 𝑨 ℓ} where
  -- If ψ = θ × φ is decidable, then so is φ.
  product₂-decʳ : IsProduct₂ R θ φ → 𝕌[ 𝑨 ] → (∀ x y → Dec (R x y))
    → ∀ x y → Dec (proj₁ φ x y)
  product₂-decʳ p u = product₂-decˡ (product₂-sym p) u
