    Examples.Classical.Groups.CyclicGroup6¶

    Worked example: ℤ/6ℤ ≅ ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/3ℤ as a direct decomposition¶

    This is the Examples.Classical.Groups.CyclicGroup6 module of the Agda Universal Algebra Library.

    The cyclic group of order six is the direct product of the cyclic groups of orders two and three, by the Chinese remainder theorem. This module checks that decomposition with the theory of Setoid.Decompositions, every finite fact decided by evaluation.

    • The groups ℤ/6ℤ and ℤ/2ℤ are built from their addition tables, as in Examples.Classical.Groups.CyclicGroup3, which supplies ℤ/3ℤ.
    • Reduction modulo 2 and modulo 3 are homomorphisms ℤ/6ℤ → ℤ/2ℤ and ℤ/6ℤ → ℤ/3ℤ; the homomorphism equations are decided over the finite carriers. Their kernels θ₂ (congruence mod 2) and θ₃ (congruence mod 3) are decidable congruences of ℤ/6ℤ.
    • θ₂ and θ₃ form a factor pair (ALV Definition 4.28), decided by isFactorPair?: their meet is the diagonal and their composite is total. By Theorem 4.29, ℤ/6ℤ ≅ ℤ/6ℤ/θ₂ × ℤ/6ℤ/θ₃, and by the first isomorphism theorem the two quotients are ℤ/2ℤ and ℤ/3ℤ.
    • ℤ/2ℤ and ℤ/3ℤ are directly indecomposable, by small→indecomposable (each has at most three elements, and their nontriviality is decided); so θ₂ and θ₃ are indecomposable congruences, and 0 = θ₂ × θ₃ is a factorization of ℤ/6ℤ into indecomposables. ℤ/6ℤ itself is not directly indecomposable.

    The congruence lattice of ℤ/6ℤ is the four-element Boolean lattice, which is distributive, so ℤ/6ℤ has the strict refinement property (ALV Theorem 5.17, Corollary 1) and its factorization is unique; that corollary is not formalized here.

    {-# OPTIONS --without-K --exact-split --safe #-}

module Examples.Classical.Groups.CyclicGroup6 where

open import Data.Sum.Base                           using ( [_,_]′ )
open import Data.Vec.Base                           using ( _∷_ ; [] )

    The groups¶

    The addition table of ℤ/6ℤ (entry a , b is (a + b) mod 6), its negation, and the group, with the five group axioms decided over the carrier Fin 6.

    -- The addition-mod-6 table.
z6-table : Table 6
z6-table = (0F ∷ 1F ∷ 2F ∷ 3F ∷ 4F ∷ 5F ∷ [])
         ∷ (1F ∷ 2F ∷ 3F ∷ 4F ∷ 5F ∷ 0F ∷ [])
         ∷ (2F ∷ 3F ∷ 4F ∷ 5F ∷ 0F ∷ 1F ∷ [])
         ∷ (3F ∷ 4F ∷ 5F ∷ 0F ∷ 1F ∷ 2F ∷ [])
         ∷ (4F ∷ 5F ∷ 0F ∷ 1F ∷ 2F ∷ 3F ∷ [])
         ∷ (5F ∷ 0F ∷ 1F ∷ 2F ∷ 3F ∷ 4F ∷ [])
         ∷ []

-- Addition mod 6.
_+₆_ : Fin 6 → Fin 6 → Fin 6
_+₆_ = ⟦ z6-table ⟧

-- Negation mod 6.
neg₆ : Fin 6 → Fin 6
neg₆ 0F = 0F
neg₆ 1F = 5F
neg₆ 2F = 4F
neg₆ 3F = 3F
neg₆ 4F = 2F
neg₆ 5F = 1F

-- The group ℤ/6ℤ.
z6-group : Group
z6-group = eqsToGroup (Fin 6) _+₆_ 0F neg₆
  (from-yes (Associative?   _+₆_)) (from-yes (LeftIdentity?  _+₆_ 0F))
  (from-yes (RightIdentity? _+₆_ 0F)) (from-yes (LeftInverse?   _+₆_ 0F neg₆))
  (from-yes (RightInverse?  _+₆_ 0F neg₆))

    The same for ℤ/2ℤ, whose negation is the identity.

    -- The addition-mod-2 table.
z2-table : Table 2
z2-table = (0F ∷ 1F ∷ []) ∷ (1F ∷ 0F ∷ []) ∷ []

-- Addition mod 2.
_+₂_ : Fin 2 → Fin 2 → Fin 2
_+₂_ = ⟦ z2-table ⟧

-- Negation mod 2.
neg₂ : Fin 2 → Fin 2
neg₂ x = x

-- The group ℤ/2ℤ.
z2-group : Group
z2-group = eqsToGroup (Fin 2) _+₂_ 0F neg₂
  (from-yes (Associative?   _+₂_)) (from-yes (LeftIdentity?  _+₂_ 0F))
  (from-yes (RightIdentity? _+₂_ 0F)) (from-yes (LeftInverse?   _+₂_ 0F neg₂))
  (from-yes (RightInverse?  _+₂_ 0F neg₂))

    The underlying Sig-Group algebras, and their finiteness witnesses: each carrier Fin n enumerates itself.

    -- The underlying algebras of ℤ/6ℤ, ℤ/2ℤ, and ℤ/3ℤ.
𝒁₆ 𝒁₂ 𝒁₃ : Algebra {𝑆 = Sig-Group} 0ℓ 0ℓ
𝒁₆ = proj₁ z6-group
𝒁₂ = proj₁ z2-group
𝒁₃ = proj₁ z3-group

-- They are finite algebras.
𝑭₆ : FiniteAlgebra 𝒁₆
𝑭₆ = record { _≟_ = _≟_ ; card = 6 ; enum = λ i → i ; enum-sur = λ x → x , refl }

𝑭₂ : FiniteAlgebra 𝒁₂
𝑭₂ = record { _≟_ = _≟_ ; card = 2 ; enum = λ i → i ; enum-sur = λ x → x , refl }

𝑭₃ : FiniteAlgebra 𝒁₃
𝑭₃ = record { _≟_ = _≟_ ; card = 3 ; enum = λ i → i ; enum-sur = λ x → x , refl }

    Reduction mod 2 and mod 3¶

    Reduction modulo 2 sends x to the parity of x, and reduction modulo 3 to its residue; both respect addition and negation, which is decided over the carrier, and they preserve the identity on the nose.

    -- Reduction mod 2.
mod₂ : Fin 6 → Fin 2
mod₂ 0F = 0F
mod₂ 1F = 1F
mod₂ 2F = 0F
mod₂ 3F = 1F
mod₂ 4F = 0F
mod₂ 5F = 1F

-- Reduction mod 3.
mod₃ : Fin 6 → Fin 3
mod₃ 0F = 0F
mod₃ 1F = 1F
mod₃ 2F = 2F
mod₃ 3F = 0F
mod₃ 4F = 1F
mod₃ 5F = 2F

-- Reduction mod 2 respects addition and negation (decided).
mod₂-+ : ∀ x y → mod₂ (x +₆ y) ≡ mod₂ x +₂ mod₂ y
mod₂-+ = from-yes (all? λ x → all? λ y → mod₂ (x +₆ y) ≟ mod₂ x +₂ mod₂ y)

mod₂-neg : ∀ x → mod₂ (neg₆ x) ≡ neg₂ (mod₂ x)
mod₂-neg = from-yes (all? λ x → mod₂ (neg₆ x) ≟ neg₂ (mod₂ x))

-- Reduction mod 3 respects addition and negation (decided).
mod₃-+ : ∀ x y → mod₃ (x +₆ y) ≡ mod₃ x +₃ mod₃ y
mod₃-+ = from-yes (all? λ x → all? λ y → mod₃ (x +₆ y) ≟ mod₃ x +₃ mod₃ y)

mod₃-neg : ∀ x → mod₃ (neg₆ x) ≡ z3-neg (mod₃ x)
mod₃-neg = from-yes (all? λ x → mod₃ (neg₆ x) ≟ z3-neg (mod₃ x))

    The two reductions are homomorphisms of Sig-Group algebras: the interpretation of ∙-Op applies the table to the two arguments, so compatibility with each operation symbol is one of the decided equations.

    -- Reduction mod 2 is a homomorphism ℤ/6ℤ → ℤ/2ℤ.
mod₂-hom : hom 𝒁₆ 𝒁₂
mod₂-hom = mkhom 𝒁₆ 𝒁₂ F λ {f} {a} → compatible {f} {a}
  where
  F : Func 𝔻[ 𝒁₆ ] 𝔻[ 𝒁₂ ]
  F ⟨$⟩ x = mod₂ x
  F .Func.cong = cong mod₂

  compatible : compatible-map 𝒁₆ 𝒁₂ F
  compatible {∙-Op}   {a} = mod₂-+ (a 0F) (a 1F)
  compatible {ε-Op}   {a} = refl
  compatible {⁻¹-Op}  {a} = mod₂-neg (a 0F)

-- Reduction mod 3 is a homomorphism ℤ/6ℤ → ℤ/3ℤ.
mod₃-hom : hom 𝒁₆ 𝒁₃
mod₃-hom = mkhom 𝒁₆ 𝒁₃ F λ {f} {a} → compatible {f} {a}
  where
  F : Func 𝔻[ 𝒁₆ ] 𝔻[ 𝒁₃ ]
  F ⟨$⟩ x = mod₃ x
  F .Func.cong = cong mod₃

  compatible : compatible-map 𝒁₆ 𝒁₃ F
  compatible {∙-Op}   {a} = mod₃-+ (a 0F) (a 1F)
  compatible {ε-Op}   {a} = refl
  compatible {⁻¹-Op}  {a} = mod₃-neg (a 0F)

    The factor pair¶

    The kernels of the reductions are congruence modulo 2 and congruence modulo 3. They are decidable, by comparing residues, so they are decidable congruences (DecCon), and isFactorPair? decides that they form a factor pair: an element is determined by its residues mod 2 and mod 3, and every pair of residues occurs.

    -- Congruence mod 2 and congruence mod 3, the kernels of the reductions.
θ₂ θ₃ : Con 𝒁₆ 0ℓ
θ₂ = kercon mod₂-hom
θ₃ = kercon mod₃-hom

-- They are decidable.
θ₂ᵈ θ₃ᵈ : DecCon 𝒁₆ 0ℓ
θ₂ᵈ = θ₂ , λ x y → mod₂ x ≟ mod₂ y
θ₃ᵈ = θ₃ , λ x y → mod₃ x ≟ mod₃ y

-- ⟨θ₂ , θ₃⟩ is a factor pair of ℤ/6ℤ (decided).
θ₂θ₃-factorPair : IsFactorPair θ₂ θ₃
θ₂θ₃-factorPair = from-yes (isFactorPair? 𝑭₆ θ₂ᵈ θ₃ᵈ)

    Neither member of the pair is the total congruence, so ℤ/6ℤ is not directly indecomposable: 0 and 1 differ mod 2, and 0 and 1 differ mod 3.

    -- Neither factor is trivial.
θ₂-proper : ¬ Trivial (𝒁₆ ╱ θ₂)
θ₂-proper t = 0≢1+n (t 0F 1F)

θ₃-proper : ¬ Trivial (𝒁₆ ╱ θ₃)
θ₃-proper t = 0≢1+n (t 0F 1F)

-- So ℤ/6ℤ is not directly indecomposable.
z6-decomposable : ¬ IsDirectlyIndecomposable 0ℓ 𝒁₆
z6-decomposable di = [ θ₂-proper , θ₃-proper ]′ (factor-trivial di θ₂ θ₃ θ₂θ₃-factorPair)

    The decomposition ℤ/6ℤ ≅ ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/3ℤ¶

    Theorem 4.29 turns the factor pair into the isomorphism ℤ/6ℤ ≅ ℤ/6ℤ/θ₂ × ℤ/6ℤ/θ₃. The reductions are onto, so by the first isomorphism theorem the two quotients are ℤ/2ℤ and ℤ/3ℤ, and the product of the two isomorphisms (×ᴬ-≅) gives the decomposition.

    -- ALV Theorem 4.29: ℤ/6ℤ ≅ ℤ/6ℤ/θ₂ × ℤ/6ℤ/θ₃.
z6≅quotients : 𝒁₆ ≅ ((𝒁₆ ╱ θ₂) ×ᴬ (𝒁₆ ╱ θ₃))
z6≅quotients = factorPair→≅ θ₂ θ₃ θ₂θ₃-factorPair

-- The reductions are onto.
mod₂-onto : IsSurjective (proj₁ mod₂-hom)
mod₂-onto {0F} = Image_∋_.eq 0F refl
mod₂-onto {1F} = Image_∋_.eq 1F refl

mod₃-onto : IsSurjective (proj₁ mod₃-hom)
mod₃-onto {0F} = Image_∋_.eq 0F refl
mod₃-onto {1F} = Image_∋_.eq 1F refl
mod₃-onto {2F} = Image_∋_.eq 2F refl

-- The first isomorphism theorem identifies the quotients.
quotient₂≅z2 : (𝒁₆ ╱ θ₂) ≅ 𝒁₂
quotient₂≅z2 = FirstIsoTheorem mod₂-hom mod₂-onto

quotient₃≅z3 : (𝒁₆ ╱ θ₃) ≅ 𝒁₃
quotient₃≅z3 = FirstIsoTheorem mod₃-hom mod₃-onto

-- ℤ/6ℤ ≅ ℤ/2ℤ × ℤ/3ℤ.
z6≅z2×z3 : 𝒁₆ ≅ (𝒁₂ ×ᴬ 𝒁₃)
z6≅z2×z3 = ≅-trans z6≅quotients (×ᴬ-≅ quotient₂≅z2 quotient₃≅z3)

    The factors are indecomposable¶

    ℤ/2ℤ and ℤ/3ℤ have at most three elements and are nontrivial (decided), so they are directly indecomposable by small→indecomposable. Transporting along the first isomorphism theorem and applying Lemma 2 of ALV §5.2, θ₂ and θ₃ are indecomposable congruences: 0 = θ₂ × θ₃ is a factorization of ℤ/6ℤ into indecomposables.

    -- ℤ/2ℤ and ℤ/3ℤ are directly indecomposable.
z2-indecomposable : IsDirectlyIndecomposable 0ℓ 𝒁₂
z2-indecomposable = small→indecomposable 𝑭₂ (from-yes (2 ≤ⁿ? 3)) (from-yes (nontrivial? 𝑭₂))

z3-indecomposable : IsDirectlyIndecomposable 0ℓ 𝒁₃
z3-indecomposable = small→indecomposable 𝑭₃ (from-yes (3 ≤ⁿ? 3)) (from-yes (nontrivial? 𝑭₃))

-- So congruence mod 2 and congruence mod 3 are indecomposable congruences of ℤ/6ℤ.
θ₂-indecomposable : IsIndecomposable θ₂
θ₂-indecomposable =
  directly→indecomposable θ₂ (≅-indecomposable (≅-sym quotient₂≅z2) z2-indecomposable)

θ₃-indecomposable : IsIndecomposable θ₃
θ₃-indecomposable =
  directly→indecomposable θ₃ (≅-indecomposable (≅-sym quotient₃≅z3) z3-indecomposable)
